Shoulder load model

The Shoulder Load Index (SLI) adds three sources, scaled so the squad maximum is 100: bowling = (release speed/143)² × action factor × balls bowled; batting = 0.35 × (bat speed/118)² × balls faced; fielding or keeping = per-throw weight × throws, with a 1.5× dive-and-gather factor for keepers. Fatigue then carries over between matches with F(t+1) = F(t)·e−4.5/3 + load. Ranges are 5th–95th percentile from 2,000 Monte Carlo draws. Seasonal workloads are assumptions by role, and bands (Low, Moderate, High) are within-squad rankings, not clinical thresholds. The research paper and dataset sent with this page document every input.

Method

Ball exit speed uses a calibrated linear impact model: vout = 0.95·vbat + 0.25·vin, which folds in bat–ball restitution, mass ratio and off-centre contact losses. Carry assumes a 30° launch angle (adjustable) with a flat 28% drag reduction versus vacuum range. Calibrated so elite hitters land near 100–110 m, which matches typical IPL maximum sixes. Bowling load uses release speed as a proxy for kinetic-chain output; action class comes from reported mechanics.
